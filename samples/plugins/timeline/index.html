How Timeline works

The map. Each session is a line, time running left to right. Rings are your prompts; the small marks between them are what Claude did: an edit, a read, a command, a test run, a question for you. The newest ring shows the session's state: pulsing while it works, with a light cycle riding its line; breathing amber when it waits for your answer; white when it has just finished; still when idle. Hover a ring for the prompt and what followed; click it, or a prompt in the list below, to open the session's conversation at that prompt; click a session's name to open the session.

The look follows the app's theme, from the palette button at the top right. With Tron on, the map dresses as the Grid: a ruled black floor, light-ribbons, a light cycle on a working session's line; red under Tron: Ares, green under Matrix.

This session follows the tab you have open and lists its prompts below the line, newest first, with what Claude said or did on each.

Tokens. Claude Code notes the cost of every reply in the conversation file, so each session shows the context its last reply was sent with, out of 200k, and the tokens written in the window. The gauge turns orange past 70%, which is when a session is near compacting. Reading this costs nothing.

Summarise is the one thing here that spends tokens: it runs Claude once, headless with your own sign-in, over the conversations shown, and asks for what was done, what was decided and what's next. The header counts the calls made today. Nothing runs on its own.

Files at the bottom are the ones edited in the window, warmer with more edits; hover one for the sessions that touched it.

Taking access away. Under the palette button, Plugins › Timeline has Stop it seeing your sessions and Stop it asking Claude about your sessions.
