How Ask works

Asking. Type a question and press Enter. Claude reads the conversations of the sessions in the About scope, your prompts and what each agent answered and did, and writes an answer. It runs once per question, headless, with your own sign-in, the way Suggest a commit message does, so each question counts against your usage. It has no tools and saves nothing, so it can't change anything.

What it can tell you. What a session is doing or waiting on, what it last changed, which session touched a file, how long ago something happened. When the conversations don't show it, it says so. Long conversations are cut at the top, newest turns kept.

The sessions it names appear as chips under each answer. Click one to open that session.

Typing into a session. The box at the bottom left chooses where your text goes. Ask the assistant sends it to Claude. Type into … puts it into that session's terminal as if you had pasted it, for you to finish and send; tick and send it to have Enter pressed for you. Type into… under an answer does the same with the answer. Ask never types into a session that has stopped at a question needing your answer.

Drafting a message. Ask "write a message for Billing asking it to commit and stop", then use Type into… on the answer. Keep what goes into a plain shell to one line: a shell runs each line as it arrives, while Claude and Codex take several lines as one paste.

Keys. Enter sends or types; Shift+Enter starts a new line.

Taking access away. Under the palette button, Plugins › Ask has Stop it seeing your sessions, Stop it asking Claude about your sessions and Stop it typing into your sessions.
